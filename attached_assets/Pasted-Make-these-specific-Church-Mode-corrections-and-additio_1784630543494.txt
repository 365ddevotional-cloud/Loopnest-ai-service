1. MULTI-PERSON INVITATION CODES
==================================================

CURRENT REQUIREMENT

Church invitation codes must not be limited to one person.

A church leader may generate one invitation for:

• General Church Membership
• Youth Group
• Women’s Ministry
• Men’s Ministry
• Choir
• Workers
• Prayer Team
• Leadership
• Children’s Ministry
• A custom church group

The same invitation code may be used by multiple people until:

• The invitation expires
• The church leader deactivates it
• The optional maximum-use limit is reached

Default Max Uses:

Unlimited

Do not default invitations to one use.

Allow the church leader to choose:

• Unlimited uses
• A custom maximum number of approved members

Examples:

• Unlimited
• 10 people
• 25 people
• 50 people
• 100 people

Clearly display:

• Number of people who have used the invitation
• Maximum uses, or “Unlimited”
• Remaining uses, where limited
• Expiry date
• Invitation status

Only approved or successfully completed memberships should count toward the use limit. Do not waste an invitation use because somebody opened the link but did not finish joining.

Use server-side enforcement so two people cannot exceed a limited invitation at the same time.

11. MESSAGE PRIVACY
==================================================

A private message from one member to the pastor must not be visible to other church members.

Allowed viewers for a private pastoral message:

• The sending member
• The assigned pastor
• An explicitly assigned authorized church administrator or pastoral assistant

Do not allow every church administrator to automatically read all private pastoral or counseling conversations.

The Church Owner may assign specific staff as:

• Pastoral Assistant
• Counselor
• Message Administrator

Only assigned participants may access the conversation.

Group messages may be read only by members of the targeted group and authorized senders.

Church-wide messages may be read by all active members of that church.

One church must never access another church’s messages.

12. ENCRYPTION REQUIREMENTS
==================================================

Do not falsely label the system “end-to-end encrypted” unless true end-to-end encryption is fully implemented.

At minimum, provide:

• HTTPS encryption in transit
• Secure encrypted database/storage where supported
• Server-side authorization
• Strict participant-based access controls
• No message content in public logs
• No message content in analytics
• No message previews exposed to unauthorized users
• Audit logging for privileged administrative access

If true end-to-end encryption is implemented:

• Only the sender and explicitly designated participants may possess decryption access.
• A pastor’s assistant can read a conversation only when added as a participant.
• The server must not be able to read plaintext.
• Key management, device recovery, lost-device handling, and multi-device access must be properly designed.
• Do not implement a weak homemade encryption system.

Because true end-to-end encryption is a major cryptographic feature, prefer secure private messaging with encryption in transit, encryption at rest where supported, and strict role-based access during this task unless the project already contains a reviewed E2EE architecture.

In the UI, call it:

“Private Church Messaging”

Do not call it “End-to-End Encrypted” unless technically verified.
